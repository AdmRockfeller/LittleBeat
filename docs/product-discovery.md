# NapNook — Discovery Findings & Product Opportunities

**Document version:** 0.2  
**Product phase:** Discovery  
**Evidence status:** Secondary research completed; primary research pending.

## 1. Key Discovery Insight

Tracking newborn activities can help parents organize information and understand their baby's routine. However, the effort required to maintain detailed records can become another source of pressure.

NapNook should not encourage parents to track everything.

Instead, it should help parents capture relevant information naturally, with minimal interruption to their caregiving routines.

**Core opportunity:**

Transform fragmented information about a newborn's daily routine into an organized, accessible and easy-to-review shared memory, without requiring parents to stop what they are doing to complete forms.

## 2. Emerging Problems and Product Opportunities

| Problem | Secondary research signal | NapNook opportunity |
|---|---|---|
| Forgetting important events | Reports of sleep-deprived parents struggling to remember details | Low-friction voice capture |
| Manual tracking effort | Repeated typing and data entry in tracking workflows | Natural-language input and AI extraction |
| Fragmented caregiver information | Parents sharing responsibilities and exchanging updates | Shared parenting memory |
| Disorganized information | Notes and records spread across different tools | Automatic chronological timeline |
| Appointment preparation | Difficulty recalling historical details during healthcare consultations | Structured, factual summaries |
| Tracking-related anxiety | Some parents experience tracking as an additional obligation | Optional, pressure-free recording |

**Interpretation:** These are emerging themes from secondary research. Their frequency and importance among NapNook's target users still need to be investigated.

## 3. Product Opportunities and Initial Prioritization

### Priority 1 — Voice-first capture

Example:

"The baby had 60 ml of formula at 2 AM and then had a wet nappy."

NapNook converts the natural-language input into structured events.

Potential benefits:
- Reduced manual entry.
- Faster event capture.
- Less reliance on memory.
- More natural interaction during nighttime care.

**Open question:** Does voice capture solve a frequent and meaningful problem, or is it simply an attractive feature?

### Priority 2 — Shared parenting memory

Both parents can understand the baby's recent activities without repeatedly asking each other for updates.

Potential benefits:
- Better continuity between caregivers.
- Less information loss during handovers.
- More consistent records.

**Open question:** Do parents need real-time synchronization across separate devices, or would a shared primary device be sufficient?

### Priority 3 — Automatic daily summaries

NapNook organizes recorded events into a concise daily overview.

Potential benefits:
- Easier access to historical information.
- Reduced effort when reviewing the baby's routine.
- Better visibility into recorded activities.

### Priority 4 — Healthcare appointment preparation

Generate factual summaries of recorded activities and parent-reported observations.

Potential benefits:
- Easier recall during appointments.
- Better organization of relevant information.
- Clear separation between recorded facts and missing information.

NapNook must not diagnose conditions or replace professional medical assessment.

## 4. Important Product Principle: Tracking Without Pressure

An important emerging insight is that tracking can become burdensome.

NapNook should therefore:

- Avoid reminders that create guilt or pressure.
- Never require parents to record every event.
- Allow incomplete records.
- Make manual correction simple.
- Avoid interpreting missing records as evidence that an event did not happen.
- Prioritize usefulness over the number of recorded events.

**Product principle:**

NapNook should reduce the mental burden of parenting, not create another task to manage.

## 5. Research Questions

Three critical questions remain unanswered:

1. Do parents actually want to record every event, or only meaningful activities and unusual observations?
2. Does voice-based capture solve a frequent problem compared with existing tracking methods?
3. Do parents trust an AI system to transform natural-language descriptions into structured records?

Additional questions:

4. How important is sharing information between two separate devices?
5. What information do parents consider useful during healthcare consultations?
6. At what point does tracking become stressful rather than helpful?

## 6. Exploratory User Research Plan

**Target:** 2–4 parents of newborns or young infants.

| Participant | Desired profile | Main research objective |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Parent of a 0–4-week-old baby | Understand early tracking routines |
| P2 | Parent currently using Huckleberry or another tracking app | Understand existing app limitations |
| P3 | Parent who stopped tracking | Understand reasons for abandonment |
| P4 | Parent sharing caregiving responsibilities | Understand information handovers |

### Interview script

**Opening**

"I'd like to understand how you currently manage and remember your newborn's daily routine. I'm not selling anything, and I'm particularly interested in what you actually do today."

1. Walk me through how you keep track of your baby's day.
2. How do you remember when your baby last fed, slept or had a nappy change?
3. Can you tell me about the last time you forgot something important about your baby's routine?
4. Do you currently use an app, notes, spreadsheets or anything else to record this information?
5. What's the most frustrating part of keeping track of everything?
6. How do you and your partner communicate about what happened during the day or night?
7. Have you ever struggled to remember information when a GP, midwife or public health nurse asked about your baby's routine?
8. If you could change one thing about how you currently manage your baby's routine, what would it be?

**Research rule:** Do not present NapNook before understanding current behavior. Ask about actual past experiences, tools and frustrations rather than hypothetical willingness to use a new app.

## 7. Initial Product Hypothesis

**NapNook can help parents reduce the effort of remembering and organizing newborn activities by turning natural-language input into structured, accessible records.**

This hypothesis is supported by emerging secondary research themes, but is not yet validated through direct user interviews.

## 8. Evidence and Research Sources

The following sources informed the initial secondary research:

1. [JMIR Pediatrics — Understanding the Implications of mHealth Technology in Collaborative Care Programs (2025)](https://pediatrics.jmir.org/2025/1/e70936/PDF) — qualitative research relevant to parental experiences with digital health technology.
2. [JMIR Pediatrics — Usability and Acceptability of a Digital Health App (2026)](https://pediatrics.jmir.org/2026/1/e86139/PDF) — relevant usability and acceptability research.
3. [The Guardian — You can track everything](https://www.theguardian.com/lifeandstyle/2019/mar/02/apps-that-track-babies-and-give-data-to-tech-firms-parents) — journalistic coverage of baby-tracking applications and parental data concerns.
4. [Reddit — Did tracking your newborn help you?](https://www.reddit.com/r/newborns/comments/1x7ec60/did_tracking_your_newborn_help_you_or_make_it/) — anecdotal parental experiences.
5. [Reddit — Tracking is making me CRAZY](https://www.reddit.com/r/NewParents/comments/10bpa4u) — anecdotal experiences of tracking fatigue.
6. [Digital Child — Key Findings on Baby Apps](https://digitalchild.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Key-Findings-Baby-apps.pdf) — research context on baby-related applications.

**Evidence limitations:**
- Academic studies provide useful context but may involve different populations and products.
- Journalism and Reddit discussions provide qualitative signals, not representative evidence.
- Secondary research cannot establish demand for NapNook specifically.
- All sources should be reviewed in full before making precise claims about sample sizes or findings.

## 9. Discovery Exit Criteria

Discovery will be considered sufficiently complete for MVP definition when:

- 2–4 exploratory interviews have been conducted and documented.
- Current parental tracking behavior is understood.
- The most important user pain points are ranked.
- The voice-first hypothesis has been assessed.
- The shared-device versus multi-device requirement is clarified.
- Privacy expectations are documented.
- The MVP problem and scope are agreed upon.
